It's been a while since I've taken any networking classes so I've been doing refreshers. Subnetting and CIDR especially.

One thing I do remember with a CIDR, the larger the number following the '/' (class C defaults to /24), the less leasable IP addresses available. However, what I did seem to forget was where the /24 came from.

But alas, the refresher helped me remember binary maths plays a part in CIDR.

255.255.255.0/24 subnet mask is the default for class C. The 24 denotes 24-bits, AKA the starting 3 octets of 255.255.255.0, or in binary, 11111111.11111111.11111111.00000000. 

When it comes to leasable IPs, 2 are reserved for the network/broadcast.

/24 = 254 leasable IPs 

/25 = 126 leasable IPs

/26 = 62 leasable IPs

/27 = 30 leasable IPs

/28 = 14 leasable IPs

/29 = 6 leasable IPs

/30 = 2 leasable IPs

/31 = no leasable IPs, this only works for a direct connection between two gateway devices
